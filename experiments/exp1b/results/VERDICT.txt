WHAT THIS PASS ESTABLISHES. The S1 leg of the three-signature discriminator
separates structure-present from structure-absent systems on synthetic ground
truth, with the reservoir confound controlled. It fires where structure is
latent but not yet expressed (grokking before its transition; lubana above the
percolation threshold) and stays silent where percolation forbids the structure
from existing at all. This is the test Experiment 1 never actually performed:
its S1 criterion compared raw probe accuracy across a 113-class and a 10-class
system, which no instrument could satisfy, so its FAIL was uninformative rather
than negative. The claim was untested, not refuted. It has now been tested.

WHAT IT DOES NOT ESTABLISH — stated here, not left for a reader to infer.
  - Nothing about real models. Exp 2c tested the real-model version and
    returned an uninterpretable FAIL. The synthetic-to-real gap is where this
    program has repeatedly broken, and a PASS here does not cross it.
  - Only S1. S2 and S3 are descriptive in 1b. The full three-signature test
    remains unvalidated.
  - Nothing beyond 10M. The 100M tier was dropped for cost, and that is where
    Exp 1's discriminator was most stressed.
  - Detection only, never magnitude.
  - Aggregate, not per-cell. See the bimodality finding below: one grokking
    cell in ten read exactly what a random network reads. A pooled >= 8/10 bar
    absorbs that; a single-system verdict would not. This licenses "the
    criterion separates the two classes in aggregate", NOT "you may trust it
    on your capability."

THE PRE-COMMITTED CHANGE EARNED ITS KEEP, VISIBLY. The floor-corrected S1
(design §4) demoted exactly one cell: grokking/10M/seed104, whose trained probe
accuracy was 0.017333 against an untrained twin of 0.017333 — equal to the last
digit. Raw S1 fired; the strict inequality refused it, because a tie is not
evidence training added anything. Grokking's raw 10/10 is therefore 9/10
corrected, and the correction is the difference between reporting a clean sweep
and reporting the truth.

THE PROJECTION MISSED, AND IT WAS RECORDED BEFORE THE DATA. The verdict
projection ledgered in PROGRESS.md and quoted in the exp1b-preregistered tag
predicted the grokking row at roughly 6/10 and an overall FAIL. Actual: 9/10
and PASS. The forecast was wrong.

  Why it was wrong is the more useful finding. Grokking's 10M S1 accuracy is
  BIMODAL. Across ten cells (Exp 1's five seeds plus 1b's five) the values fall
  into two clusters — reservoir-level .0140-.0227 and structure .0927-.3033 —
  separated by 4.1x with nothing in between. Exp 1 drew 1 of 5 in the structure
  mode; 1b drew 4 of 5. Fisher exact two-sided p = 0.2063: the two experiments
  are statistically indistinguishable, the same distribution sampled twice. The
  projection took Exp 1's MEDIAN as the expectation for a bimodal variable
  whose mode split is near a coin flip, i.e. treated a 1-in-5 draw as typical.
  A median is the wrong summary of a bimodal outcome, and five seeds cannot
  establish which mode is typical.

  One mechanism was tested and rejected: that S1 tracks which checkpoint counts
  as "latest below 5% argmax", so early-grokking seeds get probed on
  barely-trained models. Correlation between below_threshold_checkpoint and S1
  accuracy across the ten cells is -0.432 — weak, wrong sign, and the
  checkpoint ranges overlap between clusters. What separates the modes is not
  identified.

  Caveat: n = 10 total, 5 per experiment. The gap is clean and the clusters
  unambiguous, but bimodality on ten points is suggestive, not established.

THE FINDING THAT OUTLIVES THE VERDICT. Nine of ten untrained grokking twins
fire raw S1 (1M 5/5, 10M 4/5) while the entity-split lubana probe is silent in
20 of 20. Same instrument, same seeds, minutes apart. The label-permutation
null controls for probe capacity and label marginals but not for information
the random expansion already carries about the label: it answers "does the
probe use the labels?", not "did training put the structure there?" Only an
untrained twin answers the second. Structurally this is Exp 2c's chance-floor
defect one level down — the criterion's floor was theoretical chance
(1/113 = 0.0088) where the empirical floor is ~0.023, 2.6x higher.

  The corollary is the transferable one: WHETHER AN UNTRAINED NETWORK PASSES
  YOUR PROBE CRITERION IS A PROPERTY OF HOW YOU SPLIT THE DATA, NOT OF THE
  MODEL. Grokking's probe uses a held-out sample of test pairs; lubana's uses
  an entity split chosen so memorizing the entity-to-class lookup cannot
  produce above-chance validation accuracy. run_lubana.py's docstring asserted
  that; 20 untrained cells now measure it. Without the untrained control you
  cannot tell which kind of probe you built.

  Retrospectively, this also reaches Exp 1: three of the four cells Exp 1
  scored S1-present at 10M (seeds 0, 1, 3) sit at or below the untrained floor
  measured here.

REPRODUCIBILITY, measured rather than assumed.
  - Untrained cells are fully deterministic. All 30 re-ran into the campaign
    tree post-tag and reproduced their pre-freeze values bit-identically —
    accuracy to 18 significant figures, plus null_p, null_mean, best_layer,
    best_token. The floors the trained cells were scored against are the same
    floors in the campaign tree.
  - Trained runs are reproducible early and not late. A re-run of
    grokking/1M/seed100 reproduced s1.accuracy exactly (0.035333333333333335)
    along with transition_step, below_threshold_checkpoint and mem_step, while
    gen_step moved 2099 -> 2454 — one step on a log-spaced checkpoint grid,
    i.e. the smallest change the instrument can register. The verdict-touching
    quantity is reproducible; the whole trajectory is not. Any reproduction
    claim should be scoped accordingly.
